YAGNI: The Art of Building Less

Why writing less code today often leads to better software tomorrow

YAGNI stands for You Aren’t Gonna Need It. It is a software design principle from Extreme Programming that advises developers to avoid adding functionality until it is actually required.

Many developers fall into the trap of anticipating future needs. While this feels responsible, it often results in unused code, increased complexity, and harder maintenance.

Why YAGNI Works

Requirements change. Features that seem obvious today may become irrelevant tomorrow. By implementing only what is necessary now, teams reduce waste and keep their systems adaptable.

YAGNI encourages simplicity. Simpler systems are easier to test, easier to refactor, and easier for new developers to understand.

Practicing YAGNI

Practicing YAGNI does not mean ignoring the future. It means trusting refactoring and good design practices to handle change when it actually arrives.

Build the simplest solution that solves today’s problem. When tomorrow’s problem appears, solve it then—with clarity and intent.
